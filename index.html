What you can ask for

Everything below works by voice, or typed into the box under the core.

Music, shows and videos

Play a song or video on YouTube, start an exact Netflix episode, open a show in Stremio, or control Spotify. Pause, skip and jump to a minute.

  • Play Breaking Bad season 3 episode 2 on Netflix
  • Stop the song

Spotify works with the Spotify app installed and signed in, free or Premium. No keys needed.

Documents, written for you

Describe what you want and he writes it in Word, Pages, TextEdit, Notes or Google Docs on a Mac, or Word, Notepad or Google Docs on Windows. Then edit it by voice.

  • Write a story in Word
  • Make it shorter

Apps, tabs and volume

Open any app or site, close tabs by name, and set the volume. Opening an app asks what you want next: “Open Netflix” asks what to watch.

  • Close the Gmail tab

Pictures in, pictures out

Attach, drop or paste an image, then ask about it: describe it, read its text, explain an error, compare two. Ask him to draw something and he creates it.

  • What does this error mean?

Drawing is free through Pollinations.ai. Editing a picture needs an OpenAI key.

He can use your computer

Ask for something on screen, like “click Save” or “use my computer to turn on dark mode in Chrome”. He asks first, works one checked step at a time, and stops the moment you move the mouse.

How Jarvis works

An engine that listens, thinks and acts, and a window that shows what he is doing.

  1. Hear

    The wake phrase gets his attention. Your speech becomes text on your computer with Whisper, a speech model he downloads the first time he starts.

  2. Decide

    Commands he knows, like media, apps, timers and math, run in his own code. Anything else goes to his AI: llama3.2, running on your computer. A Groq key makes it faster.

  3. Act

    He drives apps through Apple Events and their shortcuts. When you ask, he uses the mouse and keyboard himself, one checked step at a time, with a Stop button on screen.

  4. Answer

    He replies with your system’s built-in voice, Hebrew included, and shows the answer, worksheet, graph or globe in his window.

What stays on your computer, and what goes online

Out of the box, the listening and the thinking happen on your computer. Here is exactly where each thing is handled.

On your computer

  • Your speech. Turned into text by a speech model on your computer. Only in the first minutes after installing, while that model downloads, does he use Google’s free recognizer.
  • Your requests. Answered by the AI on your computer (llama3.2, run by Ollama).
  • Your screen, when he uses your computer. He reads the window through your system’s accessibility features. Screenshots are only ever looked at by his local vision model and never leave your computer.
  • His voice. Replies are spoken by your system’s built-in voices.
  • Conversation logs. Saved as text files in his data folder. You can turn them off in Settings.
  • WhatsApp messages. Read from WhatsApp’s local data, only spoken and shown. Never sent to an AI or written to the logs.
  • Google Calendar sign-in. Kept in a file in his data folder.

Sent to online services

  • Only if you add a Groq key: your speech and requests go to Groq, which answers faster. When he uses your computer, the list of buttons and fields in that window goes too, never the contents of password fields.
  • Pictures you ask about go to Groq’s vision model if you added a key. Without one, his local vision model reads them, on computers with 16 GB of memory.
  • Image prompts go to Pollinations.ai, or to OpenAI if you add a key.
  • Place names and weather are looked up with Open-Meteo.
  • The first start downloads his AI engine from GitHub and his models from Ollama and Hugging Face.
  • Spotify and Google services are contacted only when you use those features.

You can change this in Settings

What you need

No Python, no terminal and no account. Just the computer.

On every computer

  • A microphone and speakers. Pick the microphone in Settings if you have more than one.
  • 8 GB of memory. With 16 GB he can also read pictures and the screen with his own vision model.
  • Free disk space for his AI: about 5 GB on Windows and 3 GB on a Mac, downloaded on the first start. His vision model adds 3 GB on computers with 16 GB of memory.
  • An internet connection for the first start. After that, listening and answering work offline. Music, the web and the weather still need it.

Windows

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. No administrator rights needed.
  • Google Chrome for Classroom and account routing. Tab control also works in Edge, Brave and Firefox.
  • Microsoft Word for Word documents.
  • A Hebrew voice, only if you want him to read Hebrew aloud: Settings, Time & language, Speech.

macOS

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer) and macOS 11 or later.
  • Google Chrome for tab control, Classroom and account routing.
  • Permissions when macOS asks: Microphone; Accessibility if he should use the mouse and keyboard; Automation for Chrome, Word and Notes. Full Disk Access only for WhatsApp reading.

Only for optional extras

  • Faster answers: a free Groq key, pasted into Settings.
  • Spotify: the Spotify app, signed in (free or Premium).
  • Better pictures and editing: an OpenAI key with billing credit.

Install and start Jarvis

A few clicks, once. After that, start him from the Start menu or Applications, or let him start when you sign in (Settings, This computer).

Install on Windows

  1. Run the installer

    If Windows says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway. It appears because the installer isn’t signed with a paid certificate.

  2. He installs and opens

    It takes a few seconds and needs no administrator rights. He is added to the Start menu and the desktop (as Jervis, in this version).

  3. Let him set up his AI

    The first start downloads his AI and speech model, about 3.6 GB, and keeps about 2.3 GB of it (NVIDIA graphics cards keep 1.8 GB more, to use the card). The progress is on screen, and timers, music and apps already work meanwhile. On a 16 GB computer, the first time he looks at a picture or the screen he downloads a 3 GB vision model.

  4. Say “Hey Jarvis”

    Closing the window keeps him listening in the notification area. Quit him from his icon there.

Install on macOS

  1. Open Terminal and paste this

    Terminal is in Applications, Utilities. The command downloads him, checks the download against the published checksum, puts him in Applications and opens him.

    curl -fsSL https://thejarvis-app.github.io/jervis/install-mac.sh | bash

  2. Allow the microphone

    macOS asks the first time he listens.

  3. Let him set up his AI

    The first start downloads his AI and speech model, about 2.3 GB, with the progress on screen. Timers, music and apps already work meanwhile. On a 16 GB Mac, the first time he looks at a picture or the screen he downloads a 3 GB vision model.

  4. Say “Hey Jarvis”

    Closing the window keeps him listening in the menu bar. Quit him from his icon there.

Installed from the .dmg instead? Drag Jervis to Applications and open him. macOS says “Apple could not verify Jervis”, because he isn’t notarized by Apple yet: click Done, open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll down to “Jervis was blocked”, click Open Anyway and enter your password. You only do this once.

To let him use the mouse and keyboard, turn on Jervis in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Accessibility. macOS asks the first time you want him to. For pause, resume and jump in Chrome, turn on View, Developer, Allow JavaScript from Apple Events.

If something goes wrong

Windows or macOS won’t open him

The warning is about the missing paid signature. On Windows: More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, install with the Terminal command above, which avoids the prompt; or, for the .dmg, click Open Anyway in System Settings, Privacy & Security. To be sure you have the real file, compare its checksum with the one under Verify the download.

My antivirus says the file is suspicious

Antivirus programs are careful with new programs that aren’t signed with a paid certificate yet, and Jarvis is one. Avast and AVG may say they are checking a file; that takes a few hours, and then it’s let through. To be sure your copy is the real one, compare its checksum with the one under Verify the download. Version 1.0.2 and later no longer run the old version’s uninstaller when updating, which is what Avast stopped in 1.0.1.

The installer says “Jervis cannot be closed”

An older copy was still running. Version 1.0.1 and later close him by themselves. With an older installer, right-click his icon in the notification area and choose Quit Jervis, or end Jervis and jervis-backend in Task Manager, then click Retry.

Setting up the AI failed or stopped

The chip at the top of his window says what went wrong and has a Try again button. The usual causes are too little disk space, or a firewall or VPN blocking the download. The download picks up where it stopped. If you already use Ollama, he uses yours instead of downloading his own.

He doesn’t hear me

Choose your microphone in Settings, Voice. On a Mac, check that he is on in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone. Check that he isn’t muted: the window shows Muted, and M turns the microphone back on.

Answers are slow

His AI runs on your computer, so a busy or older computer answers more slowly. Closing heavy apps helps. For fast answers, paste a free key from console.groq.com into Settings, AI.

Something else

In Settings, This computer, Open log files shows what he was doing, and Restart Jervis’s engine fixes most stuck states. The logs never contain what you said or typed.

More to set up

Let him use the mouse and keyboard

Ask for something on screen, like “click Save” or “use my computer to turn on dark mode in Chrome”. He asks first, then works step by step: he reads the window, does one thing and checks the result. His small local AI manages short, simple tasks; with a free Groq key in Settings he handles longer ones.

  • Always visibleA glowing edge and a bar with Pause and Stop stay on screen while he works.
  • Instant stopMove the mouse, press Ctrl+Alt+Q (Control-Option-Q on a Mac), or say “stop”.
  • Asks firstBefore sending, buying, deleting or installing anything. He never types into password fields.
OpenAI, Google Calendar and Spotify keys

All optional, in Settings, Optional services. Turn on Show advanced settings to see the Spotify and Google fields.

  • SpotifyNot needed: he uses the Spotify app. Keys (developer.spotify.com, redirect URI http://localhost:8888/callback, Premium) only add jumping to a minute on Windows.
  • OpenAIBetter image generation, and editing existing pictures.

School and personal Google accounts

If each account has its own Chrome profile, enter the profile names in Settings, Optional services, and he opens things in the right one. Say “for school” or “personal” to settle it, or “wrong account” to fix one.

Questions

What is Jarvis?

A voice assistant for Windows and macOS. You talk to him, or type, and he plays music and shows, opens apps and sites, writes documents, sets timers, solves and graphs math, answers questions, and uses the mouse and keyboard when you ask.

Is it Jarvis or Jervis?

The same assistant: he used to be called Jervis and is now Jarvis. Version 1.0.5, today’s download, still shows the old name on your computer and answers to both “Hey Jarvis” and “Hey Jervis”. The web address keeps the old name too.

Does Jarvis cost anything?

No. There is no account and no key to buy: his AI runs on your computer. One optional extra costs money: OpenAI image generation needs billing credit. Spotify works with a free account, and a Groq key for faster answers is free.

Which computers does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), and Macs with Apple silicon on macOS 11 or later. Intel Macs can run him from the source code. A few features differ: reading WhatsApp and Classroom works on macOS only, and on Windows pause and resume use the media key.

Does Jarvis need an internet connection?

For the first start, to download his AI. After that, listening and answering work offline. Music, videos, the web, the weather and Google features still need the internet.

Can he really use my computer? Is that safe?

Yes, when you ask. He works one step at a time and checks each result, and you always see it: a glowing edge and a bar with Pause and Stop stay on screen. Moving the mouse, Ctrl+Alt+Q or “stop” takes control back at once. He asks before sending, buying, deleting or installing anything, and never types passwords. Settings can make him ask before every task, or turn it off. With his small local AI he manages short, simple tasks; for longer ones, add a free Groq key in Settings.

Which AI models does it use?

On your computer: llama3.2 for answers, Whisper for speech, and qwen2.5-vl for pictures and the screen on computers with 16 GB of memory. With a Groq key: gpt-oss-20b and Whisper on Groq, which are faster.

Where are my settings and conversations stored?

In his data folder: %APPDATA%\Jervis on Windows, and ~/Library/Application Support/Jervis on a Mac (the next version moves it to “Jarvis” by itself). Settings, This computer, Open data folder opens it. WhatsApp messages are never written there.

How do I update or uninstall him?

To update, install the new version over the old one: your settings and history stay. To uninstall on Windows, use Settings, Apps, Jervis, Uninstall. On a Mac, quit him and drag Jervis from Applications to the Trash. His data folder stays until you delete it.
